How headlines are filed and scored

Filing. A headline is filed under a stock only when it names the company or its ticker. Words that could mean something else ("apple", "shell", "reliance") also need a market word nearby, such as "shares" or "earnings". Open Why? on any headline to see exactly which words filed it.

Tone. Each headline is checked against a fixed list of positive and negative finance words ("beats", "upgrade", "plunges", "lawsuit"…). More positive words than negative makes it positive, and the other way round makes it negative. "Not" in front of a word flips it, and questions are not counted. It is a count of words, not a prediction.

Order. Stocks are listed by how much was written about them, never by how positive the news was, so the page never reads as a ranking of what to buy. A stock with no headlines shows "none", which is not the same as neutral news.
